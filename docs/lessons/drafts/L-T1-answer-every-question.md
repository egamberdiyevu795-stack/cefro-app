## 1. Why this lesson

Shown at the top, filled in from the result:

> You did not answer Question {n}: "{question text}". {evaluator note for Question {n}}

## 2. Explanation

In Part 2 you get three questions and two minutes. Every question you leave out lowers your band, however good your
English is. If you answer two questions out of three, the highest band you can get is 3.

Use your minute of preparation to write one short note for each question. Then speak in the same order: Question 1,
Question 2, Question 3. Start each answer with a sentence that clearly answers that question, so the listener knows
where you are. Watch the time. After about 40 seconds on one question, move on to the next one. A short answer to
every question is better than a long answer to one.

*(112 words)*

## 4. Speaking drill

Record each prompt, listen back, then tick the checklist. Self-check only, no AI scoring.

| # | Prompt | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | In one sentence, say who gave you advice and what situation you were in. | 15 s |
| 2 | In one sentence, say what the advice was and whether you followed it. | 15 s |
| 3 | Say one sentence that moves from your story to your opinion ("As for young people in general…"), then give your opinion. | 20 s |
| 4 | Answer all three questions in 30 seconds, about one sentence for each. | 30 s |

**Checklist**

- [ ] I said something for every question.
- [ ] Each answer started with a sentence that answers that question.
- [ ] I said whether I followed the advice.

## 5. Retry the same task
